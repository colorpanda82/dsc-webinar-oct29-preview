Why this webinar

Nobody handed you a blueprint

You were handed a building.

The contracts were already written. The pay model was already chosen. The schedule, the visit length, the referral pathways, the software, the terms on which you meet a patient, all of it was decided before you arrived, by people whose interests were not yours and who never had to explain themselves to you.

Then you were taught to read all of that as the weather. Background conditions. Just how medicine works.

It is not weather. It is architecture, and somebody drew it.

Two foot and ankle surgeons saw that and walked out of it, nine years apart.

Dr. Grace Torres-Hodges opted out of every third-party payer in 2013, when there was no term for what she was doing, no directory, and nobody who had done it first. Dr. Tea Nguyen did it in 2022, having opened Pacific Point Podiatry in 2018 as an insurance-based practice, done everything the consultants told her to do, and found it was too much work for too little pay.

Same specialty. Same decision. A decade of difference in what existed to help them make it.

That gap is the conversation.

What this hour covers

What they will talk about

  • The default and the intentional design. Four things get shaped either by you or by someone else: your career path and ownership, your payment model, your boundaries and schedule, and your patient relationships. Grace shows both versions side by side.
  • Where your frustration is actually coming from. Not the inbox. Not the schedule. The contract and incentive structures underneath them.
  • The wedge. What third-party payers actually do to the relationship at the center of your work, for you and for the patient, in plain structural terms.
  • The procedural problem, twice. A monthly membership makes sense for chronic management. It does not obviously work when your work is surgery. Grace solved it one way in 2013. Tea solved it another way in 2022. Both are in the room.
  • The four pillars of a protected practice. Intentionality, autonomy, agency, and boundaries. Boundaries treated as structural protection rather than a lifestyle luxury.
  • Two interventions, one timeline. Prevention for the physician who has not signed yet. Reclamation for the one who signed years ago.
  • What each would do differently. Tea asks Grace. Then the room asks both.

The physicians

Who is in the room

Dr. Grace Torres-Hodges, DPM, MBA, FACPM, FASPS

Board-certified foot and ankle surgeon, practicing in Pensacola, Florida, where she founded her solo private practice in 2001. A second generation physician in her own community, she cares for patients of all ages across Northwest Florida and South Alabama.

Diplomate of the American Board of Podiatric Medicine. Fellow of the American College of Podiatric Medicine and of the American Society of Podiatric Surgery. Undergraduate at Vanderbilt, residency at St. Vincent's Medical Center, MBA from Quantic.

In 2013 she was introduced to direct care and free market medicine and opted out of all third-party payers. She co-leads the Florida chapter of the Free Market Medical Association and has spoken at the Association of American Physicians and Surgeons and Docs 4 Patient Care. She hosts lectures and roundtables with medical students and residents from Florida State, the University of Florida, Kent State, Des Moines University, Eglin Air Force Base, and Navy Hospital Pensacola.

A former national spokesperson for the American Podiatric Medical Association, she is a frequent source on foot health for Prevention, Good Housekeeping, the Wall Street Journal, and NPR. She writes weekly on Substack as The Direct Doctor.

Author of two books: Private Practice Solution, winner of the Independent Press Award for Medicine and an International Impact Award in Business, and Doctor Do, Doctor Don't, released June 2026. She does not discount them, because direct care does not discount its services. Since 2023 every dollar of proceeds has sponsored students to attend free market and direct care conferences. books.by/drgracedpm

Dr. Tea Nguyen, DPM

Board-certified podiatric physician and surgeon. Doctor of Podiatric Medicine, Des Moines University. Diplomate of the American Board of Podiatric Medicine. International visiting scholar in plastic and reconstructive microsurgery at Chang Gung Memorial Hospital, Taiwan.

She founded Pacific Point Podiatry in Santa Cruz County in 2018, the first podiatry practice in the county built on a patient-centric, root-cause model, and took it entirely outside insurance in 2022. She is the founder and surgical director.

A co-founder of the DSC Alliance and one of the four physicians who started the direct specialty care movement, she hosts The Direct Care Podcast for Specialists, serves as Chief Wellness Officer of the Direct Care Society, and coaches specialists through the move to direct care, including a twelve-week teaching module on building the practice.

Her line from the August webinar, on the most expensive mistake physicians make: "A medical degree is not the brand. It is how you show up and talk to people."

FAQ

Who is this for?

Board-certified specialist physicians at any stage. The physician who has not signed their next contract yet, and the one who signed years ago and has been living with it.

Is it free?

Yes, the live hour is free for any physician interested in direct care. The live room holds 100.

Can I send a question in advance?

Yes. There is a field on the registration form. The best questions go to Grace and Tea first, before the open floor. The specific ones win, about your own contract or your own numbers, not the model in general.

What if I cannot attend live?

Register anyway. The recording lives in the member library, so if that matters to you, your first month of membership is free and it takes the same two minutes.

Do I need to have left insurance already?

No. A good share of the room every month is still employed and still deciding.

Is this specific to podiatry?

No. Both physicians are surgeons, which makes the procedural side concrete rather than theoretical, but the argument is structural: who shapes your contracts, your pay model, your boundaries, and your patient relationships.

Can I get her books?

Yes, directly at books.by/drgracedpm. Since 2023 all proceeds sponsor students to attend free market and direct care conferences.
